A letter from somewhere in the world 06 / 09 / 2026

To those who find their way here,

We have been trying to find the right way to tell you what this is.

Not what it sells, exactly. That part is easy enough. There are clothes, accessories, objects, materials, things made by hand and things made with machines.

What is harder to explain is why we wanted to make a place for them in the first place.

Perhaps it begins with the feeling of finding a place that somebody did not make for everybody.

A place you hear about from another person. An address written on a piece of paper. A road you have never taken before. A door that is not particularly remarkable from the outside.

Sometimes the road is part of the invitation.

You go because somebody you trust told you that there was something worth seeing there.

Maybe they could not explain it very well. Maybe that was part of the reason you went.

We have always liked those kinds of discoveries.

The things that are not presented to you all at once. The good restaurant at the end of an unremarkable road. The old house you notice only because somebody points it out. The person who knows exactly where to find the right material. The tree that has grown in a place where it seems it should not be able to grow.

There is a particular pleasure in being let in on something rather than being sold something.

That is the kind of place we wanted to make.

Somewhere to come without having to know exactly what you are looking for.

You might come because you heard about a coat. You might come because you wanted to see how something was made. You might come because you were passing through. You might come because somebody told you there was a person here who knew about leather, or cloth, or wood, or some strange material you had never heard of before.

And you might leave without buying anything.

We are quite comfortable with that.

Because the conversation is part of it.

So is the looking. So is touching something and turning it over to see how it has been put together.

We like people who ask what something is made of. People who notice a seam. A button. The weight of a fabric. The way a pocket has been placed. The way an object feels after it has been used for a few years rather than five minutes.

Not because everything needs to be explained, but because knowing how something came to exist can change the way you look at it.

This is also why we spend so much time with materials.

Sometimes the right thing already exists. Then the sensible thing is to find it, understand it, and leave it alone.

Sometimes it does not exist.

Then we have to make it.

Before a thing has decided what it wants to become.

This is where things can become rather slow.

A material behaves differently than expected. A construction looks right on paper and wrong when it is worn. Something works perfectly except for one small thing that becomes irritating every time you use it.

So we change it.

Then we make it again.

And sometimes we realise that the first version was better after all.

We do not think this is wasted time.

Time is part of the material too.

We have become suspicious of the idea that everything should happen quickly.

Quickly chosen. Quickly made. Quickly photographed. Quickly replaced.

There are things for which speed is wonderful. This does not have to be one of them.

Some ideas need to sit around for a while. Some decisions become obvious only after you have gone home. Some materials need another attempt. Some conversations are better continued a month later.

We would rather leave room for that.

There is a difference between making something slowly and simply making something inefficiently. We are interested in the first. The point is not slowness for its own sake. The point is giving enough attention to know when something is right.

And when something finally leaves, we do not really think of the work as finished.

It goes with somebody.

It gets worn. It gets packed into a bag. It gets taken onto a train, into a car, through an airport, into a house, out into the rain.

Something gets spilled on it. Something gets repaired. A pocket becomes the pocket where somebody always puts their keys.

Slowly, almost without noticing, the thing stops being ours.

It becomes part of somebody else's life.

Things become interesting when they are used.

We like that very much.

We do not want to make things so precious that they become afraid of being used.

If something needs repairing, bring it back. If it needs changing because your life changed, let us see what can be done.

We would much rather repair a good thing than watch it disappear because of something small.

And the marks that cannot be repaired— those are different.

We like those.

Not artificial ageing. Not things manufactured to look as though they have travelled when they have not. Just the quiet evidence that something has actually been somewhere.

Perhaps that is also why the world around things matters to us almost as much as the things themselves.

We like photographs that feel like photographs, rather than advertisements pretending to be them.

Skin that looks like skin. Light that has simply arrived. A face that remains a face. Good proportions. A little imperfection left where it belongs.

We like fields. Trees. Endemic plants. Old houses. Roads. Weather. Walls that have changed colour over time. Places where you can tell that people have been living there for a very long time.

Things that do not need to announce themselves.

The things around us are part of the picture too.

There is a place we keep imagining.

Not because we have decided exactly what it should be, but because the image keeps returning.

A piece of land somewhere. A house that already has a history. Perhaps an old colonial house, perhaps something much simpler.

Endemic plants around it. Trees that were there before us. A little weather. A long table. Materials. Books. Clothes. Tools. Somewhere to work. Somewhere to sit. Somewhere to have a conversation that unexpectedly lasts three hours.

It could become a workshop. It could become an office. It could become something closer to a salon. Or perhaps none of those words would be quite right.

The point is not the name.

The point is having somewhere that allows all these things to exist together: making, thinking, meeting, travelling, collecting, learning, and occasionally doing absolutely nothing.

We imagine that the people who find this place will probably have something in common.

Not a particular age. Not a particular profession. Not a particular way of dressing.

Something harder to describe.

They may have travelled with very little. They may have crossed a country by car, slept somewhere uncomfortable, eaten somewhere they could not pronounce, learned a useful thing from a stranger, or changed their plans because somebody told them about a road worth taking.

They may come from one place and somehow belong to several.

They may speak several languages. They may know how to spend an afternoon doing nothing at all.

They may understand why a tree matters without needing the explanation.

They may notice a beautiful object immediately, and then want to turn it around to understand why.

Curious, but not restless. Adventurous, but comfortable with very little. Interested in the world without needing to decorate themselves with it.

Those are the people we hope to meet.

Although perhaps the best thing is that we would not know them from a description.

We would know them because the conversation would probably continue longer than expected.

We would show them something. They would tell us about something. They would ask a question we had not thought of. We would make some coffee. Somebody would notice a material on the table. And somehow an afternoon would disappear.

Somewhere between making a place and finding one.

Maybe that is what we are really trying to build.

Not simply a collection of things, and not simply a place to buy them.

An address.

A point on the map where a certain kind of curiosity can find other curiosity.

A place where a good object can sit beside a piece of material, beside a photograph, beside a book, beside a story from somewhere far away, without any of them needing to explain why they belong together.

A place where making something well is enough of a reason.

A place where buying something is not the only possible outcome of coming through the door.

A place where things can take the time they actually require.

We do not know exactly where all of this will lead.

That is perhaps the part we like most.

We are still looking for materials. Still making mistakes. Still finding people. Still travelling. Still changing our minds. Still discovering that something we thought was important was not, and something we nearly ignored was.

There will be clothes. There will be objects. There will be photographs, materials, places, conversations, and probably a few things we have not yet thought of.

We hope there will always be room for those.

So, if somebody gives you this address, come when you can.

You do not need to know what you are looking for.

Come and look around. Ask questions. Sit down. Tell us where you have been. Tell us what you have found.

Perhaps we will have something to show you. Perhaps you will show us something instead.

Either way, we will be glad you came.

Until then,
