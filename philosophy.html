From the workshop 06 / 09 / 2026

To those who find their way here,

We thought we would write a little about why this place exists.

It is difficult to explain what we do without making it sound more organised than it really is.

There are clothes here. There are accessories. There are materials and machines, patterns and samples, things being tested, things being taken apart, and quite a lot of things on tables.

But the things themselves are only part of it.

Somewhere things are being made

We wanted an address.

The kind of address someone gives you after you have been talking for a while.

Perhaps about a road they travelled, somebody they met, a coat that has been with them for twenty years, a strange little place to eat, a tree growing somewhere it should not, or a material they found by accident.

They write the street down on a piece of paper. You put it in your pocket. They tell you that you should go there.

You do not know exactly what you will find.

We like that.

We also believe that some things are worth experiencing rather than simply acquiring.

When you come, you come by appointment.

There is no rack of things waiting for you to choose from in five minutes.

We talk first.

We try to understand something about your personality, the way you live, the things you notice, what you need, and perhaps what you did not know you were looking for.

You touch the materials. You look closely. You take your time.

You may leave without anything.

That is completely fine.

You might come back another day.

We talk again.

Perhaps a cloth is right but the colour is not. Perhaps something needs to be developed. Perhaps we need to make a material ourselves. Perhaps someone elsewhere has already made exactly what we need, and we would rather use theirs than pretend otherwise.

There is no reason to hurry an answer simply because a calendar says we should.

A few months can be a perfectly reasonable amount of time for something to become right.

Materials before they become something else

We are very interested in the product itself.

The construction. The proportion. The behaviour of a material. The way something folds. The way it moves. The way it wears. What happens after one year, five years, ten.

We like good gear. Good tools. Good materials. Things whose quality is not dependent on explaining how expensive they were.

Sometimes we develop something in house because it should exist and does not.

Sometimes we search a very long way for something someone else already knows how to do exceptionally well.

The point is not novelty. The point is not complexity. The point is to get closer to something good.

And then, eventually, the piece leaves us.

It goes into your life.

You wear it. You travel with it. You use it. You spill something on it. You repair something else while wearing it. You forget about it. Then you put it on again.

Slowly, it becomes yours.

Not because we made it look old. Not because we manufactured a patina.

Because time happened to it.

We like the marks that arrive honestly. The quiet relief of use. The evidence that something has accompanied a person rather than simply survived in a cupboard.

And if something happens to it, come back.

If it needs altering, come back.

If it needs repairing, come back.

If it is one of our pieces, we want that relationship to remain.

We would rather fix a good thing than replace it because replacing it is easier.

We are also fascinated by images.

Not images that shout that they are images.

A good natural image. Good proportions. Skin that looks like skin. Soft skin. Relief. Light that feels as though it was already there.

We like documenting things because sometimes a photograph reveals an idea that was hiding in plain sight.

We like fields. Trees. Endemic plants. Old houses. Roads. Weather. The strange beauty of ordinary places.

Things that were already growing there

Somewhere in our heads there is a place: a workshop, perhaps an office, on a large piece of land, surrounded by plants that belong to that place, in an old house with another history.

We do not know if it will ever exist exactly like that.

But we like the wave created by putting these things together.

The basic beside the extremely considered. Something very old beside something newly developed. A good object beside a tree. Work beside stillness.

We like strange combinations when they make their own sense.

And perhaps there are people who understand this without needing much explanation.

People who have travelled without needing much money to do it.

People who have taken a backpack, a car, a road, and simply gone.

People who have endured enough experience to recognise quickly when something is worth knowing.

People who understand why trees matter.

People with more than one origin, or several languages, or several places they call home.

People who know that good things take time to make.

People who like touching materials, trying things, understanding how they work, and seeing what happens when an idea becomes physical.

Adventurous, but not restless. Curious, but not hungry for novelty. Extremely natural in daily life. Comfortable with very little. Interested in good things without needing to make a performance of them.

Plastic bodies are not our DNA.

Perhaps that is the simplest way to say it.

We are interested in the real thing.

The material. The person. The place. The work. The time. The marks left by living.

We believe a good object should be allowed to stay with someone for a very long time.

And perhaps that is why we wanted this place to feel less like a shop and more like somewhere you arrive.

A small oasis in a rather bizarre world.

Somewhere to breathe.

Somewhere time can become suspended for a little while.

You come in. You talk. You touch something. You look at the light. You leave without having to buy anything.

And perhaps, months later, something made together finally comes home with you.

We do not think everyone needs this.

But we think the people who do will probably understand.

If you are one of them, perhaps someone will give you the address.

Until then,
