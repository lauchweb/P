A letter 06 / 09 / 2026

To those who find their way here,

There are places that are difficult to explain. Usually, the best way to find them is through someone else: a name mentioned in conversation, an address written down, a quiet recommendation from someone whose taste you trust.

Arbre Nuage began with that feeling. The feeling that something good does not always need to announce itself, and that finding it should still involve a little curiosity.

At the centre of it are clothes and objects, but the objects are only the beginning. There are conversations, materials, books, photographs, tools, ideas, mistakes and things still being figured out.

The intention is quite simple: to make and find things that are good enough to keep.

A visit usually begins with a conversation.

Not necessarily a conversation about what someone wants to buy. More often, it is about how they live, what they carry, where they go, what they use every day, and the things that have stayed with them for a long time.

Sometimes the answer is obvious. Sometimes it takes another visit. Sometimes nothing is needed at all.

That is perfectly alright.

A cloth might feel right but need a different colour. A proportion might need changing. Something might need to be made from the beginning. Or perhaps someone else has already made exactly what is needed, and the better decision is simply to use it.

There is no particular virtue in making everything ourselves. What matters is knowing why something is there, how it works, and whether it deserves to exist.

Good things tend to reveal themselves slowly.

A piece can look right on paper and feel wrong when worn. A material can behave differently from what was expected. A small detail can become obvious only after living with something for a while.

So things are changed. Then changed again. Sometimes they are left alone for a few months before the answer becomes clear.

This is not something to apologise for. Attention takes time.

The same is true once something leaves the studio. A good object should be allowed to enter ordinary life. It should be worn, carried, used, packed, travelled with, forgotten for a while and found again.

Eventually, it becomes less ours and more yours.

That is when it becomes interesting.

The marks of use are part of that process. A softened edge. A change in colour. Something repaired rather than replaced. Nothing manufactured to look old; simply the evidence of having been lived with.

If something needs altering or repairing, it can come back.

The relationship should not have to end when the purchase is made.

There is also a certain way of looking at things that matters here.

A good object does not need to be loud. Neither does a good place, a photograph, a piece of clothing or a person.

Beauty can be quite quiet. A good proportion. The right light. A particular kind of weather. An old wall. A road after rain. Something familiar seen from a slightly different angle.

This is perhaps why Arbre Nuage sits somewhere between clothing, objects, images and place. They are different things, but they often begin with the same instinct: to look a little closer.

The people who find their way here are likely to understand that instinct.

They may have travelled a great deal or hardly at all. They may have more than one home, several languages, or simply a habit of following their curiosity.

They notice things.

The construction of a jacket. The way an object feels in the hand. A building at the end of a road. Something made decades ago that still works exactly as it should.

There is no particular type of person required. Only a certain openness to looking, asking, touching, learning and changing one's mind.

Perhaps, eventually, there will be a physical place for all of this.

A workshop, a house, a table, a few rooms, somewhere to work and somewhere to sit. A place where objects, clothes, books and ideas can exist beside one another without needing to justify the connection.

Somewhere people can stay longer than they planned. Somewhere a conversation can take an afternoon. Somewhere there is no particular pressure to arrive at an outcome.

For now, it is enough to begin with the things themselves and the people around them.

To make something when it is worth making. To find something when it has already been made well. To repair what can be repaired. To keep looking.

And to leave enough room for whatever comes next.

Perhaps that is all there is to say for now.

If someone has given you the address, come and see for yourself.

There is no particular way to arrive. Come curious. Stay a while.

Until then,
Arbre Nuage
