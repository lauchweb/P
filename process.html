Arbre Nuage

There are places you hear about from someone you meet somewhere.
A letter from somewhere

Maybe someone gave you our address.

Not online. Not because you searched for us. Someone simply told you about the place.

Perhaps they wrote the street down on a piece of paper.

You were talking about travelling. About a place they had been. About something they had seen, touched or understood there.

And then they said: you should go there.

So you came.

We like the idea that not everyone will understand the address.

Not because it is secret. It is simply not for everyone.

Arbre Nuage is an address.

A place where you can come to make something with us.

Clothing. Accessories. Materials. Time.

You come by appointment.

You might leave without anything.

First,
we meet.

There is no need to arrive knowing exactly what you want.

We talk.

We look at things.

You touch the materials. We talk about how they feel, where they came from, what they might become.

We talk about you, too.

Your proportions. Your habits. Your travels. What you wear. What you don't wear. What you have kept for years.

Slowly, something begins to take shape.

Materials worth touching
We are interested in the thing itself.

We are a passionate team, but perhaps not the usual one.

We spend a great deal of time on product development, materials, construction and good gear.

Sometimes we develop materials ourselves.

Sometimes we look for someone who has spent a lifetime becoming exceptionally good at one thing.

Sometimes the right material is already there.

We are not interested in making something appear exceptional.

We want it to actually be exceptional.

In the hand. On the body. After ten years.

Time
stays.
A piece may take a few months.

That is not a problem we are trying to solve.

The time is part of it.

There is time to change something. To test it. To make it again. To wait. To talk again.

Then one day you return.

And perhaps you finally experience the extension of what we started together.

Something that has become deeper than the first idea.

Then it becomes part of your life.

We don't want our pieces to remain perfect in the way objects sometimes remain perfect when nobody uses them.

Wear them. Travel with them. Work in them. Sleep somewhere strange in them. Take them somewhere we have never been.

Let them collect marks.

Let them soften. Let them change.

We like patina when it comes from time and wear.

Not from pretending something is old.

If something happens to your piece, come back.

We will fix it. We will alter it. We will work on it again.

If it is ours, you can come back.

Maybe you already know why.

You may have travelled without much money.

By backpack. By car. On foot. Somehow.

You may have spent time in fields, villages, workshops, roadsides, other people's homes.

You may speak several languages. Or come from several places. Or carry several histories with you.

Maybe you understand quickly when something is worth knowing.

Maybe you understand why trees matter.

Maybe you like touching things before deciding what you think about them.

Maybe you are adventurous, but don't particularly need to look adventurous.

You like beautiful things. But you also like basic things.

You want to live naturally.

You don't need everything around you to tell people who you are.

Perhaps that is enough.

We are fascinated by the world around the things we make.
A workshop and office somewhere in a large piece of land, surrounded by endemic plants, in an old traditional house.

We like the wave that creates.
We like images that feel like they happened rather than images that were constructed to look natural.

Good proportions. Skin that looks like skin. Softness where softness exists. Relief. Weather. Light.

We like documenting things because sometimes an image makes us notice an idea we hadn't seen before.

The tree and the cloud.

Two things we cannot really leave life without.

Trees and clouds are part of the cycle that brings rain, growth and life.

We like the idea that there are things we can live without owning, but not without experiencing.

Perhaps a good life is partly the search for those things.

Somewhere in the middle of this strange world, a place to take a big, good breath.

We don't know exactly what will happen when you come.

That's part of why we would like you to.
