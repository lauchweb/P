Process

It usually begins with looking. An image, an object, a place, a colour, a mark, or something almost unnoticed can be enough to start an idea.

I like to leave room for that idea to change. Drawing can lead to an image, an image can lead somewhere else, and something unexpected can become more interesting than the original thought. The process is not always linear, and it does not need to be.

Experimentation is part of the work. I make, look again, remove, repeat, combine, and sometimes start over. Different materials and forms offer different ways of thinking, and moving between them keeps the process open.

The finished piece is only part of what interests me. What happens along the way matters too — the accidents, the changes, the things discovered by doing rather than planning. I try to recognise those moments and let them take the work somewhere of its own.
